# Regional Transit Connections

## Purpose and ownership

A read-only management tab in Master Schedule, immediately after Platforms. The approved GO-focused chart puts each GO train trip in a column and each published local route/direction in a row. Separate grids show **To GO** and **From GO** for Allandale Waterfront and Barrie South. This is not an editable Connection Library, optimizer, trip planner, or dispatch surface.

The view reads current published master versions through the existing master-schedule service and selects local day type using the Master Schedule calendar's holiday service settings. An explicit service date selects GO trips from their actual GTFS calendars and exceptions. Current master versions are reference snapshots, not reconstructed historical versions; a future effective date must not be applied to earlier dates.

## Data sources and trust boundaries

- Local data: existing team-scoped published entries and Storage payloads, including supported read-only partner sources. No new Firebase collections, queries, writes, or permission grants.
- Regional data: the fixed public Metrolinx GO static GTFS ZIP. The browser fetches the public feed without credentials and parses it off the main UI thread. The result is cached in memory only; no PDF-derived or legacy fallback train times are permitted.
- Display feed fetch time, selected date and calendar coverage. Master versions and technical trip identifiers are kept in cell details, not grid headings. Network failures, expired/out-of-coverage feeds, unmapped stops, malformed timing and failed master reads remain disclosed in source notices and cell details.
- Filter to rail service and actual agency/station stop IDs. Preserve source identifiers for details; train columns show only station departure/arrival times. Calendar removals and additions override weekday flags. Pickup/drop-off restrictions must not create ordinary connections to prohibited boarding/alighting events.
- Master station matching uses the exact codes configured in `utils/platform/platformConfig.ts`, plus approved nearby curbside codes scoped to `utils/regional-transit/connectionAnalysis.ts`. Allandale includes Stop 14 (Essa at Gowan), across the street, for Route 12B and any other published trip serving that code. This does not add Stop 14 to terminal platform assignments; sources and matched-cell details disclose the crossing, without deducting or estimating walking time. Broad name matches must not silently attach a route to a GO station. Stop pairing is disclosed in connection details; walking paths and accessibility are not verified.
- The app's existing fixed-route access rules still govern master reads. Public GTFS contains no team data. Read-only analysis never changes schedules, targets, drafts, roles, rules or GTFS.

## Connection calculation

For every active GO train event, choose the nearest eligible local trip at a configured station stop:

- **To GO:** train departure minus local arrival.
- **From GO:** local departure minus train arrival.
- Imported masters may store arrival in both `stops` and `arrivalTimes`; when those clocks agree, add the documented per-stop recovery for From GO departure (numeric `stopMinutes` departures remain authoritative). A recorded interline arrival may precede the outgoing trip start only when its documented stop recovery resolves exactly to that start/departure. Preserve that explicit arrival for To GO where an inbound active segment exists; do not reconstruct or infer preceding interlined trips.
- Explicit local arrivals take precedence; otherwise subtract per-stop recovery from the recorded departure. Legacy trip-level recovery belongs after the trip and must not make intermediate station timing unassessable; at an intermediate timepoint without explicit dwell, arrival equals the recorded stop time. Missing/invalid explicit arrivals and ambiguous terminal recovery remain unassessed. A rejected station arrival keeps the direction unassessed rather than claiming the remaining trips are complete; cell details identify the first rejected trip, stop, raw arrival/departure values, trip bounds and recovery evidence for diagnosis.
- For older sparse trips without explicit partial-trip bounds, a station outside the first/last recorded stop span (at least two represented stops) is unserved by that trip and is skipped, not treated as missing station timing for the entire direction. Missing/malformed timing inside the span remains unassessed; explicit partial-trip bounds always take precedence. No missing clocks are interpolated and no short trip is extended. Timing validation is scoped to trips with an eligible station call; invalid active bounds remain disclosed. Rejected trip bounds include the first rejected trip ID, raw start/end anchors, stop indexes and direction table size so weekend failures can be diagnosed without guessing an overnight repair.
- Preserve service-day minutes beyond midnight. Arrival must remain separate from departure/recovery. Partial local trips cannot use stops outside their active range.
- To GO requires an inbound active bus segment; From GO requires an onward active segment. A terminal departure is not onward passenger service. No continuation between separate trips is inferred, and ambiguous repeated stop calls remain unavailable.
- Raw scheduled gaps use the approved chart bands: 1–5 minutes **tight**, 6–15 **comfortable**, 16–30 **long**. No positive gap within the 30-minute window means **no connection** only when timing data is complete; absent or ambiguous data means **unavailable**, not failure or success.
- Hide whole routes with no qualifying 1–30 minute connection across the selected station/date/direction view. If one direction connects, keep all direction rows of that route. Source and timing warnings remain disclosed even for hidden routes; an empty view does not prove absent service.
- Count all underlying assessed route-direction rows, including hidden routes, not unique routes or riders. Keep train columns even if none of the rows has a qualifying connection.
- These are timetable opportunities. Walking/boarding time is not deducted; the view explicitly says so. It does not establish guaranteed transfers, on-time reliability, accessibility, demand or fare eligibility.
- Transit On-Demand has no fixed schedule and is not counted as a timed connection. Other regional providers are outside this initial GO implementation.

## Interaction and verification

Station/date/direction controls and source refresh are read-only. Cell details identify the local trip/stop, train trip and timing math. Mobile grids scroll horizontally with route labels retained; keyboard users can operate the controls and close details. Printing is limited to the connection chart.

Weekday/Saturday/Sunday buttons select a nearby matching service date, not an independent override of the local timetable. Prefer the current/next matching date within two weeks; if the GO feed ends before it, use a recent covered matching date within two weeks. Local holiday service overrides are respected. The visible service date drives both providers; if no matching covered date exists, coverage errors remain explicit.

Full-screen mode fits all train columns to the available desktop width and closes with Escape. Each grid has its own sticky station/date/direction header: To GO remains visible over the first grid, and From GO replaces it at the second grid. GO time headings remain visible beneath that header on fit-width grids; wide/mobile grids retain time headings inside a bounded two-axis scroll area. Route labels use configured variants (for example 12A/12B), otherwise actual direction labels; failed sources retain configured labels without inventing trips. Row headers omit version/status metadata. White cells say “No Connection” for any non-displayed connection; a visible dagger marks unassessed cells. Underlying unavailable assessments remain distinct, excluded from checked counts, and explicitly disclosed in concise notices and details. This display label never establishes absence of service when data is incomplete.

The chart follows the app's Barrie Bloom palette and Nunito typography: green primary/selected controls with dark readable text, green To GO and blue From GO context, white rounded grids, and unchanged gap-colour meanings. One compact title/action bar and filter tray precede the tables. Normal source metadata, holiday explanations, version notices and detailed interpretation live in collapsed Sources & notes; blocking failures and concise route/source warnings stay visible. The compact title identifies GO train trips with a train icon and the locally bundled official GO Transit logo; no marketing banner is introduced. Long (16–30 minute) gaps use muted light grey with readable darker grey text, so they remain visible but secondary to comfortable green connections; tight gaps retain pale yellow. Cells, legend and detail status share the same gap styles.

Refresh first rereads the authorized published master list, so newly published routes can appear, then reloads source contents and GO data. A failed list refresh must not present the old chart as current; team changes discard superseded results.

Focused domain, feed-loading, component and Master Schedule integration tests cover actual calendars, overnight service, arrival/recovery, route matching, restricted events, errors and navigation. Browser verification must use the production React component, not the earlier HTML mockup. Fixture browser checks are not authenticated live Firebase or production deployment proof. Any release remains a separate, explicitly approved operation.
